## Live failure evidence

The six game attempts received 25, 16, 32, 9, 21 and 25 frames respectively. Every
attempt observed `clip_generated`, `clip_started` and `clip_finished`. Each requested
six-second scene was accepted as 6.583 seconds, but the adapter requires 120 received
frames at 24 fps before publishing the game's five-second MP4. No capture-buffer
overflow was recorded. There were no GPU allocation timeouts or HTTP 429 errors.

A separate one-session probe reproduced the failure outside the game server. Its
WebRTC measurements during video playback showed:

- Video packet loss of approximately 17.5–22.6% in the collected snapshots.
- Round-trip time approximately 235–249 ms during playback.
- Video jitter rising to 665 ms and 106 NACK requests in the last sample.
- Only 25 frames decoded at that sample, 2–4 fps in the later windows, and zero
  decoder-reported dropped frames; the final callback count reached 27.
- No application capture-buffer overflow; the buffer received too few frames.

These observations place the immediate bottleneck in the media transport/decode
input, before the app's frame queue. They do not establish whether packet loss began
on local Wi-Fi, the intervening route or Reactor's side. Increasing model warmup time
would not restore packets lost during a completed clip's playback. The game continues
to reject incomplete captures rather than publishing a short clip as a full entry.

All seven provider sessions were independently confirmed closed. No unresolved-provider
marker remained, and `/api/session` reported `party: null` after test cleanup. The local
service remains running with FastH3 selected as Prompt Royale's live provider.
