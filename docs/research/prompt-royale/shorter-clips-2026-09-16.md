# Prompt Royale accepts shorter clips — 16 September 2026

**Live follow-up:** A subsequent [complete three-player live round](end-to-end-2026-09-16.md)
passed on this adapter with three real five-second clips, a scored winner, rematch
setup and confirmed session cleanup. The implementation checks below used fixtures
and synthetic frame delivery.

The user approved accepting shorter video when fewer frames arrive. FastH3 capture
now encodes each received image once at 24 fps, up to 120 images. This replaces the
[five-second image-hold fallback](tolerant-capture-2026-09-15.md). A 40-frame capture
produces about 1.67 seconds, without padding or interpolation.

## Implementation

- `fast_h3.py` saves frames in arrival order until its 120-frame cap or matching
  playback finish. It continues accepting frames beyond five receiver seconds when
  delivery is slow. Receiver timing is diagnostic information, not a padding schedule.
- Matching `clip_finished` and at least two valid captured frames remain required.
  Empty, single-frame, failed and unfinished playback remain unavailable. Session
  deadlines, bounded queues and independent closure are retained.
- `media.py` accepts positive-duration video and prepares a silent H.264/yuv420p MP4
  at 1280×768, up to five seconds. It no longer rejects a source or output solely
  because it is shorter than five seconds. Byte limits and media validation remain.
- FastH3's accepted generation duration can also be below five seconds. The normal
  request remains six seconds, clamped to the provider's advertised limits.
- The lobby now says “Up to five seconds on screen.” Existing arena behavior supports
  mixed lengths: shorter clips finish on their last image, then restart with the group
  after the longest clip ends. No repeated frames are inserted into their MP4s.

Motion can jump or run faster across missing source frames. This policy accepts that
tradeoff; it does not recover missing video or fix Reactor's delivery path.

## Verification

The 11 capture regression scenarios use simulated SDK events and frames from a native
thread, with the actual capture, FFmpeg preparation and ffprobe validation. Results:

| Input | Captured/output frames | Final duration | Repeated output frames |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 158 frames | 120, capped | 5.000 s | 0 |
| 40 frames | 40 | 1.667 s | 0 |
| 40 frames arriving over 13 seconds | 40 | 1.667 s | 0 |
| Provider accepts a one-second clip | 24 | 1.000 s | 0 |
| Four frames at receiver times 0, 0.5, 2 and 4 seconds | 4 | 0.167 s | 0 |
| Two frames at receiver times 0 and 4 seconds | 2 | 0.083 s | 0 |

Decoding the entire four-frame output verifies that all four colors appear once each
and in order. Other cases cover command rejection, generation failure, zero/one frame,
and cancellation before playback finishes, including partial-file cleanup and closure.

`make check` passed: **215 backend tests**, Ruff, formatting, frontend lint and build.
The updated browser test also passed scoped ESLint.

A three-browser rehearsal used the actual MP4s generated by these capture tests as
fixtures in an isolated server on port 8001. Room **3420** completed in **46.1 seconds**:

- Three eligible clips of **0.083333, 1.666667 and 5 seconds** loaded and played.
- Every clip fired its completion event, and the group repeated. At the recorded
  snapshot, the shortest clip had ended while the longer two were still playing.
- All videos were 1280×768 with no media errors.
- The host remained present with its document hidden for 35 seconds.
- Three votes produced a scored winner: Alex, with two votes; Bailey received one.
- Play again and room closure passed, and the retired code returned 404.
- Desktop arena/results and phone results screenshots were visually inspected.

This was a deterministic capture and browser regression, not a new Reactor generation
or a physical-phone test. No provider sessions were created during this change.
